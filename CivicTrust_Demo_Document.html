1. Problem Statement

Context

South Africa allocates billions of Rands annually to municipal infrastructure projects — roads, water systems, community halls, drainage, and housing. Despite this investment, the country faces a persistent crisis of tender fraud, corruption, and poor service delivery at the municipal level.

Key Issues

IssueImpact
Opaque tender processesCitizens have no visibility into how public funds are allocated, locked, or spent. Municipal budgets are managed behind closed doors.
No independent verificationContractors self-report project completion. There is no objective, automated mechanism to verify that physical work was actually done to standard.
Delayed or stolen paymentsContractors who do honest work face delayed payments. Corrupt officials divert funds before work is completed.
No citizen participationResidents living next to construction sites have no formal channel to report progress, defects, or fraud to auditors.
Ward committee rubber-stampingWard committees often approve projects without visiting sites or reviewing evidence, as there is no structured on-chain voting record.
No audit trailPaper-based records can be lost, altered, or destroyed. There is no immutable ledger of who approved what and when.
Core Problem: There is no transparent, tamper-proof, and citizen-inclusive system for managing municipal tender funds, verifying construction quality, and ensuring democratic community oversight in South African municipalities.

2. Proposed Solution

CivicTrust is a decentralized web application that places municipal tender funds into blockchain-controlled escrow and uses AI-powered photo analysis to verify construction progress before releasing payments. It introduces five key innovations:

2.1 Milestone-Based Smart Contract Escrow

When a municipality creates a tender, the full budget is locked in an Ethereum smart contract. Funds are split across milestones (e.g., foundation, walls, roofing, finishing). Each milestone's funds can only be released when it passes AI verification AND receives unanimous ward committee approval. No single person can move the money.

2.2 AI-Powered Construction Verification (Gemini Vision)

Instead of relying on contractor self-reporting, CivicTrust uses Google's Gemini Vision AI to compare "before" baseline photos with "after" citizen-uploaded photos. The AI scores the project on structural integrity, materials quality, and completion progress (0-100). A score below 75% automatically flags the milestone as disputed.

2.3 Citizen Evidence Collection

Any resident with a smartphone can open the Citizen Area Scanner (no wallet or login required), scan their GPS location to find nearby tenders, photograph the construction site, and submit their evidence. This photo is pinned to IPFS (permanent decentralized storage) and fed to the AI auditor.

2.4 Ward Committee 3-of-3 Multisig Voting

Three designated ward committee representatives must unanimously approve each milestone on-chain before funds are released. If any single rep rejects, the milestone is immediately flagged as disputed. Every vote is permanently recorded on the blockchain — no more rubber-stamping without evidence.

2.5 Full Public Transparency

Every tender, every escrow balance, every AI score, and every vote is recorded on the public Ethereum blockchain. Anyone can verify the state of any project using a block explorer like Etherscan.

In summary: Municipality locks funds → Citizen uploads evidence → AI verifies quality → Ward committee votes on-chain → Smart contract auto-releases funds to contractor. No intermediary can steal or redirect the money.

Key Architectural Decisions

  • On-chain: Escrow funds, tender state, milestone status, votes — anything that requires trustlessness and immutability.
  • Off-chain (Supabase): Photo URLs, AI report text, GPS coordinates, activity logs — data that is too large or expensive to store on-chain but still needs fast querying.
  • IPFS: Permanent content-addressed storage for photos and reports. The CID stored on-chain guarantees the file hasn't been tampered with.
  • Oracle pattern: The backend server acts as a trusted oracle — it submits citizen evidence and AI scores to the smart contract on behalf of users who don't have wallets.

7. Demo Flow & Scenario Walkthrough

Scenario: The City of Johannesburg needs to repair a gravel road in Ward 14. We will walk through the entire lifecycle — from tender creation to fund release — demonstrating each technology at every step.

Phase 1: Municipal Official Creates Tender

1 Connect Wallet
The municipal official opens the CivicTrust login page and clicks "Connect MetaMask Wallet".
Technology: MetaMask injects the Ethereum provider into the browser. ethers.js reads the connected wallet address and calls isMunicipal(address) on the smart contract to verify the role. React Router redirects to the Municipal Dashboard.
2 Create & Fund Tender
Navigate to "Create Tender". Fill in: title ("Ward 14 Gravel Road Repair"), ward, contractor wallet, GPS coordinates (-26.1907, 27.997), deadline, 3 ward rep wallets, baseline "before" photo, and milestone amounts (e.g., 0.002 ETH for foundation, 0.002 ETH for surfacing).
Technology: The baseline photo is pinned to Pinata IPFS, returning a CID. The form calls createTender(params) on the Solidity smart contract via ethers.js, sending the total ETH as msg.value. The contract validates all inputs, locks the ETH in escrow, initializes milestones, and emits a TenderCreated event. The frontend then calls /api/sync-tender to cache the data in Supabase for fast queries.
3 Verify on Dashboard
Return to the Dashboard. KPI cards show updated escrow balance and active tender count, pulled live from the smart contract via getDashboardStats().
Technology: React useEffect calls the contract's view function. ethers.js decodes the returned tuple. The activity feed is fetched from Supabase's activity_log table via the Express API.

Phase 2: Citizen Submits Evidence

4 Open Citizen Scanner (No Wallet)
A resident near the construction site opens the app and clicks "Citizen Area Scanner" from the login page. No wallet connection needed.
Technology: This route is unprotected in React Router — no RequireRole wrapper. The page renders without any blockchain authentication.
5 Scan GPS & Find Nearby Tenders
Click "Scan My Location". The browser requests GPS permission. Nearby active tenders appear as cards showing distance in meters.
Technology: HTML5 Geolocation API provides coordinates. The frontend calls /api/tenders/nearby?lat=-26.19&lng=27.99. The Express server queries Supabase for active tenders, computes Haversine distance for each, filters within 5km radius, and returns sorted results.
6 Upload Photo & Run AI Audit
Select the tender, photograph the site, add observation notes, and click "Upload Photo to IPFS". Then click "Run Gemini AI Audit".
Technology: (Upload) The photo is sent as multipart/form-data to POST /api/evidence. The Express server pins it to Pinata IPFS, stores the CID and metadata in Supabase's citizen_evidence table, and calls submitEvidence() on the smart contract via the oracle wallet.
(AI Audit) POST /api/audit fetches both the before (baseline) and after (citizen) photos, converts them to base64, sends them to Google Gemini Vision AI with a structured prompt, receives JSON scores, pins the report to IPFS, stores it in Supabase, and calls submitAIScore() on the smart contract. If score >= 75, the milestone moves to AwaitingVote. If < 75, it's flagged as Disputed.

Phase 3: Ward Committee Votes

7 Ward Rep Logs In
Switch MetaMask to a ward rep account. Connect wallet on the login page. The app detects isGlobalWardRep and routes to the Ward Committee page.
Technology: The useWallet hook calls isGlobalWardRep(address) on the smart contract. React Router's RequireRole component checks the detected role before rendering the page.
8 Review Evidence & Cast Vote
Expand the tender card to see before/after IPFS photos, Gemini AI scores (overall, structural, materials, completion), citizen notes, and the live vote status. Click "Approve" or "Reject".
Technology: Tender data is fetched via getWardRepTenderIds() then getTender() and getAllMilestones(). Vote status uses getMilestoneVoteStatus(). The Approve/Reject button calls castVote(tenderId, true/false) on the smart contract via MetaMask. If 3-of-3 approve, the contract automatically calls _releaseMilestone() which transfers ETH to the contractor and advances to the next milestone.

Phase 4: Contractor Receives Payout

9 Contractor Views Dashboard
Switch MetaMask to the contractor account. The Contractor Dashboard shows all assigned tenders, milestone progress bars, AI scores, and payout amounts.
Technology: getContractorTenderIds(address) returns the contractor's tender IDs. getAllMilestones() provides the pipeline view. Released milestones show the payout timestamp and amount. The ETH was automatically sent to this wallet address by the smart contract's _releaseMilestone() function — no claim needed.

8. Anticipated Questions & Answers

Q: Why blockchain instead of a normal database?

A normal database can be altered by administrators. The core problem we're solving is trust — municipal officials, contractors, and citizens don't trust each other. The blockchain provides an immutable, publicly verifiable ledger that no single party can tamper with. The smart contract enforces the rules automatically — funds cannot be released without AI verification AND ward committee approval. No human can bypass this.

Q: Why Ethereum Sepolia instead of Avalanche or another chain?

Sepolia is the official Ethereum testnet — it's free, stable, and has the most tooling support (MetaMask, Etherscan, Remix, ethers.js). For production, the contract could be deployed to Ethereum mainnet, Avalanche, Polygon, or any EVM-compatible chain with minimal code changes. The contract is chain-agnostic.

Q: What happens if the AI gives an incorrect score?

The AI score is one layer of verification, not the only one. If the AI scores below 75%, the milestone is flagged as disputed, and the municipality can trigger a re-inspection (reInspectMilestone()) which resets the cycle. If the AI scores above 75%, the ward committee still has to unanimously approve — they can reject if they disagree with the AI. The system is designed with multiple checkpoints.

Q: What if a ward committee member is corrupt?

Every vote is recorded on-chain with the voter's wallet address, the timestamp, and whether they approved or rejected. This creates permanent accountability. If a rep approves a clearly deficient project, their vote is publicly visible on Etherscan forever. Additionally, the 3-of-3 requirement means all three must agree — one honest rep can block a fraudulent release.

Q: How does the citizen scanner work without a wallet?

Citizens interact only with the Express backend server, which acts as an "oracle" — a trusted intermediary that submits data to the blockchain on their behalf. The citizen uploads a photo to the server, which pins it to IPFS, runs the AI audit, and calls submitEvidence() and submitAIScore() on the smart contract using its own authorized oracle wallet. The citizen never needs ETH, a wallet, or any blockchain knowledge.

Q: What if the contractor doesn't do the work?

The funds remain locked in the smart contract escrow. No work = no citizen evidence photos = no AI score = no ward committee vote = no payment. If the milestone is disputed, the municipality can reassign the contractor (reassignContractor()) or cancel the tender entirely (cancelTender()), which refunds the remaining escrow to the municipal treasury.

Q: Can someone upload a fake photo?

Several countermeasures: (1) The citizen's GPS coordinates are recorded and compared against the tender's geofence — the photo must come from near the actual site. (2) Gemini AI compares the uploaded photo against the baseline "before" photo — it will detect if the photo doesn't match the expected location or shows a completely different site. (3) The photo is permanently pinned to IPFS with an immutable CID, so it cannot be swapped later.

Q: Why use IPFS instead of storing photos in Supabase?

IPFS provides content-addressed storage — the file's hash (CID) is stored on the blockchain. This means anyone can independently verify that the photo referenced on-chain hasn't been modified. Supabase could be edited by an administrator. IPFS + blockchain together create a tamper-proof evidence chain.

Q: What is the 3-of-3 multisig and why not 2-of-3?

3-of-3 means all three ward committee representatives must unanimously approve a milestone release. This is stricter than 2-of-3 because it prevents any two colluding members from approving a fraudulent project. Any single honest representative can block a release by rejecting. For municipal public funds, this higher standard is justified — it prioritizes accountability over convenience.

Q: How would this scale to production?

For production: (1) Deploy the contract to Ethereum mainnet or a Layer 2 like Polygon/Arbitrum for lower gas fees. (2) Upgrade Supabase and Pinata to paid tiers. (3) Add a proper oracle service (e.g., Chainlink) instead of a single backend wallet. (4) Implement proper Supabase Row Level Security policies. (5) Add mobile-responsive PWA support. The architecture is already production-ready — only configuration changes are needed.

Q: What is the smart contract's gas cost?

On Sepolia (testnet), gas is free. On Ethereum mainnet, createTender costs approximately 300,000-500,000 gas (~$5-15 depending on network congestion). castVote costs ~80,000 gas (~$1-3). On Layer 2 networks like Polygon, these costs drop to fractions of a cent. For municipal budgets of millions of Rands, these costs are negligible.

Q: Why Supabase if data is on the blockchain?

The blockchain stores state (escrow amounts, vote counts, milestone status) but it's slow and expensive to query. Supabase acts as an indexed cache for fast frontend queries — filtering tenders by ward, searching by GPS proximity, loading activity feeds, and storing large text data like AI reports. The blockchain remains the source of truth; Supabase is the read-optimized layer.

Q: What prevents the oracle (backend) from submitting fake AI scores?

The oracle is a known, authorized address set by the super-admin. In production, this would be replaced with a decentralized oracle network (e.g., Chainlink Functions) where multiple independent nodes verify the AI score before submitting. For this prototype, the single oracle model demonstrates the concept while keeping costs at zero.
